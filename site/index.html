Tell it what you finished.

Bookpile lives in whatever messaging app you already have open. Say what you are reading the way you would tell a friend, and it becomes a proper record in a library you own — a folder of notes, a database file, a spreadsheet, whichever you picked.

No app to open, no form to fill in, nothing to remember at the end of a book. Everything further down this page was built from messages like these.

What happens to that message Seven steps, none of them optional
  1. 1

    Allowlist

    Your numeric ID, checked on every message. Anyone else gets silence — never an error that reveals the bot exists.

  2. 2

    Deterministic parse

    Plain commands never reach a model. An install with no AI key at all is fully functional.

  3. 3

    Validate

    Strict schema, no coercion. "today" is refused — the server decides what today is, not the model.

  4. 4

    Resolve ambiguity

    A title matching two books is a question back to you, never a guess.

  5. 5

    One named operation

    Five exist. There is no generic update and no escape hatch.

  6. 6

    Read the record back

    From storage, after writing.

  7. 7

    Reply

    Only once the read-back confirms it. You are never told something was saved that wasn't.

Natural language is untrusted input.
Model output is an untrusted proposal.
Only validated named operations may mutate storage.

Your library lives wherever you already keep things.

Bookpile does not own your data — it agrees on a shape for it. These are examples, not a menu. Anything that can hold a record and pass the conformance vectors qualifies, including whatever you are already using.

The test of success is an export and re-import, not a resemblance. Leave your implementation entirely and the library still opens.

The actual point

A menu, not a dashboard

Every other self-hosted tracker hands you the charts its authors happened to build. Bookpile ships a catalogue of metrics, each with its data requirements, its chart form and its acceptance test — and the interview asks which ones you want.

What you record determines what you can be shown. A library with nothing but titles and statuses supports four tiles and a status bar. Add dates and the time series unlock. Record where a story is set, and you reach a tier nothing else on the shelf can offer.

A build never advertises a chart its data cannot fill. An empty chart is worse than an absent one.

T0
Presence — title, author, status. Library size, status breakdown, shelves, completeness.
T1
Dates — started and finished. Per-year bars, calendar, days-to-finish, shelf time.
T2
Judgement — rating and ownership. Rating distribution, the three-way ownership split.
T3
Bibliographic — pages and publication year. Pages per period, length, era.
T4
Setting — where the story actually happens in time. The three-clocks scatter.

How correctness is defined

Bespoke builds are proven by portable given/when/then vectors, not by inspection. Each metric carries at least three: happy path, empty, and null-handling.

conformance/vectors/

What is never generated

The allowlist, intent validation, idempotency and the privacy scanner ship as fixed, tested code.

The more of a project an agent generates, the more rigidly fixed its safety layer has to be.
